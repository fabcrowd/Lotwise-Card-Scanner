## Product and goal
A personal Chrome MV3 extension (`tools/extension-wasm/`, plain-JS ESM) that identifies and prices Pokemon TCG cards from listing photos on any site (Marketplace, eBay, Mercari, HiBid, Craigslist, Poshmark, Goodwill). The owner's goal: improve **detection, recognition and pricing** from real listing photos, balancing user experience against accuracy. Owner rules, which hold throughout:
- **No price for an unconfirmed card.** Two states only: CONFIRMED or CHOOSE (image-first picking, best match preselected, one tap confirms). Printing (holo/regular/reverse) is the user's pick when inferred only from the picture.
- Stacked or overlapping cards with no clear boundary are out of scope.
- A wrong confirmation must be one-tap correctable and remembered (corrections memory, default on). Unpriceable cards (non-English, merchandise) get a "Can't price this" skip.
- Dollars lost per wrongly confirmed card and taps saved per correct card are the two scores.
- **Never read, list or search `data/sealed-test`.** Never tune to per-photo results. Run `node scripts/sealed-score.mjs` only when the owner asks.
- Never `git add -A`; stage by file name. Commit only when asked; pushes need an allowing permission rule (the permission system blocks public actions otherwise; do not work around it).
- Photos from marketplaces are other people's: keep them under gitignored `.tmp/` or `data/`, never commit. The data repo `fabcrowd/card-scanner-data` is PUBLIC.

## Research track: CLOSED 2026-10-25, all no-ship
Reports are local in `C:\repos\projects\card-scanner-data\data\reports\` (not pushed). Decision: **stop compression and QAT work. Do not ship the quality-aware rule.**
- `quality-calibration/`: quality features add nothing beyond height, cosine and lead (M3 vs M2 AURC delta -0.0003, CI spans 0). Rule M3 at the 1.5% target confirmed 303 of the 570 batch2 cards with 7 wrong (2.3%, Clopper-Pearson upper 4.3%), versus shipped 122 with 0 wrong. All 7 wrong were under 150 px. B2 (small adapter on real labels) did not improve with more labels. B1: fp32 beats the shipped int8 by about 3 points top-1 at 100-149 px.
- `quant-recovery/`: every static int8 variant is far worse than the shipped dynamic int8. fp32 (352 MB) and fp16-weights (176 MB) gain +3.9 points at 100-149 px but break the 130 MB cap and are about 1.5x slower. No winner.
- `qat/`: quantization-aware fine-tune on the RTX 4080, 3 runs, under 1 GPU hour. R3 gained +2.7 [+0.8, +4.4] points at 100-149 px at the same 89 MB size and speed, but lost 1.6 points at 300+ px against a 1.0 limit, so it fails the pre-registered rule. The near-miss file is kept locally at `card-scanner-data/train/out/qat/R3/epoch16-ema-int8.onnx`; nothing uploaded. The same int8 model run two valid ways differs by about +-2 points at 100-149 px, so 2-point gains are near execution noise.
- Still open (not part of the closed track): hypothesis R150b (R150 failed on 2026-10-25; see Labelling status). Multi-crop averaging and larger source photos stay deferred.
